# Impeccable

## Design Context

### Users
One user: a CS425 student who prepares for a paper-based final exam. Context:
focused study sessions, often at a desk with pen and paper next to the laptop.
The job to be done: learn to draw six UML artifact types by hand, correctly
and fast, then verify their own work.

### Brand Personality
Disciplined, reassuring, hand-made. The material should feel like the notebook
of a meticulous student, not like a SaaS landing page. Emotional goal: calm
confidence under exam time pressure.

### Aesthetic Direction
"Engineering notebook / exam paper." Deliberate single light theme: warm paper
surfaces, subtle graph-paper grid behind diagrams, ballpoint-blue ink accent,
pencil-gray construction marks, red pen reserved for warnings and common
mistakes. Typography: Besley for headings (exam formality), Atkinson
Hyperlegible for body (built for legibility), Kalam for handwritten margin
annotations. Anti-references: dark dashboards, glassmorphism, gradient text,
icon-topped card grids.

### Design Principles
1. Paper first: every page must print cleanly; the screen version mimics paper.
2. Show the stroke order: teach diagrams as a drawing procedure, step by step.
3. One running example everywhere: consistency across artifacts is the exam's
   own grading rule, so it is also the design rule.
4. Red ink is expensive: use it only for mistakes and warnings.
5. Nothing decorative that does not teach.
