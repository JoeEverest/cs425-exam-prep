Q1 · System use-case diagram 10 min · 8 marks

The whole system: Student and Equipment Staff; View Available Equipment, Reserve Equipment, Return Equipment, Cancel Reservation, Manage Equipment. Suitable associations and any appropriate «include»/«extend».

Why the «include»: steps 1–2 of the given Reserve Equipment flow are exactly the View Available Equipment use case, run every time. "Always happens as part of" = «include». Why no «extend»: nothing in the scenario is optional-with-a-condition. A forced «extend» costs more than an omitted one. "If cancellation is allowed" is a business rule inside Cancel Reservation, not a relationship.

  • 1 · Both actors outside the boundary.
  • 2 · All five use cases, verb + noun, inside the named boundary.
  • 3 · Associations right — including two actors on Return Equipment (the flow says "Student or Equipment Staff").
  • 1 · A justified «include» from Reserve → View, arrow pointing at the included use case.
  • 1 · No invented relationships; clean notation (no arrowheads on associations).

Q2 · Design-level sequence diagram — Reserve Equipment 20 min · 12 marks

Main flow plus the important alternative flows (equipment does not exist, equipment unavailable, invalid dates). Participants: actor, boundary/API, controller/request-handler, service, repositories, entities, database.

Model answer Q2 
sequenceDiagram
  actor S as Student
  participant A as «controller/API» :ReservationController
  participant V as «service» :ReservationService
  participant ER as «repository» :EquipmentRepository
  participant RR as «repository» :ReservationRepository
  participant EQ as «entity» :Equipment
  participant DB as Database
  note over S,V: steps 1–2 - the View Available Equipment «include» runs first
  S->>+A: reserveEquipment(equipmentId, reservationDate, returnDate)
  A->>+V: reserve(studentId, equipmentId, dates)
  V->>V: validateDates(reservationDate, returnDate)
  V->>+ER: findById(equipmentId)
  ER->>+DB: select equipment
  DB-->>-ER: row / empty
  ER-->>-V: equipment / not found
  alt dates valid, equipment found and available
    V->>+EQ: isAvailable()
    EQ-->>-V: available
    V->>+RR: save(new Reservation with status ACTIVE)
    RR->>+DB: insert reservation
    DB-->>-RR: ok
    RR-->>-V: reservation
    V->>+EQ: markUnavailable()
    EQ-->>-V: updated
    V->>+ER: save(equipment)
    ER->>+DB: update availability
    DB-->>-ER: ok
    ER-->>-V: saved
    V-->>A: confirmation with reservation details
  else invalid dates - return date before reservation date
    V-->>A: validation error
  else equipment does not exist
    V-->>A: not-found error
  else equipment unavailable
    V-->>A: unavailable error
  end
  deactivate V
  A-->>-S: response

Layer discipline: the controller receives and responds — nothing else. The service validates dates, coordinates the checks, creates the reservation, and flips availability. Repositories translate to database operations. The entity answers isAvailable() from its own status. One alt frame with an else per alternative flow keeps all three failures visible without three diagrams.

  • 2 · All seven participants present, in call order, database included.
  • 2 · Controller thin; business logic in the service only.
  • 2 · Activations opened and closed; dashed returns everywhere, back to the actor.
  • 2 · All three alternative flows shown with clear outcomes.
  • 2 · Both writes drawn: insert reservation AND update equipment availability.
  • 1 · Availability answered by the Equipment entity, not computed by the controller.
  • 1 · Every message traces to a numbered step of the given flow.

The traps in this specific paper

  • Return Equipment has two actors. The flow says "Student or Equipment Staff" — both get association lines.
  • Two writes, not one. Reserving creates the reservation AND updates equipment availability. Miss the second write and Q2 and Q3 both leak marks.
  • The controller is not the service. One receives, one decides. Merging them loses the architecture marks the question explicitly names.
  • Q1 is the whole system. The detailed flow belongs to Q2/Q3 only; the use-case diagram must show all five use cases.
