The scenario

MediBook — Clinic Appointment System. Patients book appointments at a clinic through an online portal. The system always verifies that the patient is registered before any booking. The patient picks a doctor and a time slot; the system checks that the slot is free, records the appointment, and sends a confirmation through an external SMS gateway. When the requested slot is taken, the patient can join the cancellation list for that slot instead. A receptionist can cancel any appointment. Clinic policy: only registered patients can book (BR-1); a slot holds exactly one appointment (BR-2); booking closes 2 hours before the slot starts (BR-3).

(b) Use-case specification — Book Appointment 7 min · 7 marks

Write the full specification: name, goal, primary actor, preconditions, trigger, main flow, alternative flows, exceptional flows, postconditions, business rules.

Model answer (b)
Name
Book Appointment
Goal
The patient holds a confirmed appointment in a free slot.
Primary actor
Patient
Preconditions
The patient is signed in to the portal. The doctors' schedules are published.
Trigger
The patient submits a booking request for a chosen doctor and slot.
Main flow
  1. The patient selects a doctor and a time slot.
  2. The system verifies the patient's registration [BR-1].
  3. The system checks that the slot is free [BR-2].
  4. The system checks that the booking window is open [BR-3].
  5. The system records the appointment.
  6. The system sends a confirmation through the SMS gateway.
  7. The system shows the confirmation to the patient.
Alternative flows
3a. The slot is taken. → 3a1. The system offers the cancellation list. 3a2. The patient accepts; the system records the entry. The use case ends.
Exceptional flows
2a. The patient is not registered. → 2a1. The system rejects the request and shows the reason [BR-1]. The use case ends.
4a. The booking window is closed. → 4a1. The system rejects the request and shows the cutoff time [BR-3]. The use case ends.
Postconditions
Success: an appointment exists and an SMS was sent. Alternative: the patient is on the cancellation list. Failure: nothing is recorded and the patient saw the reason.
Business rules
BR-1 only registered patients book · BR-2 one appointment per slot · BR-3 booking closes 2 hours before the slot.
  • 1 · All sections present; name matches the oval.
  • 1 · Preconditions are states; the trigger is the event. "Registered" is NOT a precondition — the flow checks it.
  • 2 · Clean happy path, no "if" inside; every branch is a lettered flow at its step.
  • 1 · Slot-taken filed as alternative (acceptable end); the two rejections as exceptional.
  • 1 · Postconditions cover all three endings.
  • 1 · Three business rules named and cited in the steps that check them.

(g) Subsystem question 4 min · 3 marks

The clinic wraps booking into a Scheduling subsystem. Give its interface with operations, and explain information hiding and dependency reduction — one sentence and one consequence each.

Model answer (g)

ISchedulingService: +bookAppointment(patientId, doctorId, slotId) · +cancelAppointment(appointmentId) · +joinCancellationList(patientId, slotId). One operation per use case — not CRUD.

Information hiding: the portal depends only on ISchedulingService, so the controller, repositories, and storage can change without any change to the portal. Dependency reduction: every client depends on one interface instead of six internal classes, and all arrows point one way — UI → interface → service → repositories.

  • 1 · Interface operations mirror the use cases.
  • 2 · Both principles stated with their consequences.
